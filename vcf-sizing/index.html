Fleet management

Host types

Define each server once, then pick it for clusters below. Only values that exist are offered: cores per socket from current Intel Xeon or AMD EPYC CPUs, memory as balanced DIMM configurations (up to vSphere's 24 TB per host). NVMe drives serve vSAN ESA and, per cluster, the memory tier. Host types are kept in this browser and travel with Copy settings.

Instances, domains and clusters

Each instance has a management domain (its first cluster runs the VCF management appliances and every workload domain's vCenter and NSX Managers) and any number of workload domains. Each cluster picks a host type and sets its own storage, protection and memory tiering.

Add-ons

Appliances run in each instance's management cluster unless stated
Applies to clusters ticked "vDefend". Distributed Firewall adds no appliances. IDS/IPS keeps 2 GB of RAM per host; its CPU comes out of the CPU ratio's headroom. Malware Prevention runs one service VM per host (4 vCPU, 6 GB, 80 GB). Security Services Platform (one per NSX Manager instance): 3 controllers (4 vCPU, 8 GB) + workers (16 vCPU, 64 GB) + 1 reserved worker + installer; Large is needed for all ATP features.
Per instance: Site Recovery (light below 1,000 VMs: 2 vCPU, 8 GB; standard 4, 12) + vSphere Replication (4 vCPU, 8 GB) + 1 replication server (2 vCPU, 1 GB) per extra 400 VMs. Licensed per protected VM. Replicas at the recovery site are workload: add them as a group there.
3-node controller cluster per instance. Service Engines are reserved 1:1. Licensed in Service Units: 1 per Service Engine vCPU.
Per instance: HCX Manager (4 vCPU, 12 GB) + Interconnect (8 vCPU, 6 GB) + Network Extension (8 vCPU, 3 GB each; HA doubles them).
Provider appliance (8 vCPU, 16 GB, 736 GB) in the first instance. Databases are workload: add them as a group. Separately licensed.

Sources and assumptions

  • Management footprint: Broadcom "VCF Fleet Sizing Models" (VCF 9.1 techdocs) totals for the first instance (VCF Management Services, SDDC Manager, vCenter, NSX, VCF Operations, License Server, Cloud Proxy, VCF Automation) and each additional instance. Without VCF Automation the Automation nodes are taken off (Small 24 vCPU / 96 GB; Medium 24 / 96; Large 32 / 128 per node). Log Management resizes the management-services workers (16 vCPU / 32 GB at Small, 24 / 48 at Medium and Large) and is estimated with one extra worker and 500 GB per replica. Operations for Networks: platform Medium 8 / 32 GB, Large 12 / 48, XL 16 / 64; collector 4 / 12, 8 / 16, 8 / 24. The same models are used for VCF 9.0.
  • Each workload domain adds its vCenter (Small 4 vCPU / 21 GB / 734 GB, Medium 8 / 30 / 933, Large 16 / 39 / 1,383, XL 24 / 58 / 2,308) and, unless it shares NSX, its NSX Managers (Medium 6 / 24 / 300 per node, up to 128 hosts and 5 clusters; Large 12 / 48 / 300, 1,024 hosts and 256 clusters; XL 24 / 96 / 400, 2,048 and 512) to the management domain. Limits: 40 workload domains per instance; 2,500 hosts per vCenter; 96 hosts per vSphere cluster, 64 per vSAN cluster.
  • Minimum hosts: vSAN 3 (stretched 6), NFS / Fibre Channel 2; the management domain's default cluster 4 with an HA model (3-node NSX anti-affinity; stretched 8), 3 on vSAN with Simple. N+# spare hosts are added on top (per site when stretched). Management vCPU is counted at the management CPU ratio (VCF guidance ≤ 2:1), NSX Edges and Avi Service Engines 1:1 (fully reserved), RAM 1:1.
  • vSAN: new clusters use vSAN ESA (every NVMe drive is capacity). Auto policy follows ESA Auto-RAID (RAID-5 2+1 at 3–5 hosts, RAID-6 4+2 at 6+). A chosen policy raises the cluster to its host minimum (ESA RAID-5 2+1 below 6 hosts, 4+1 at 6+; RAID-6 6; RAID-1 FTT=2 5). Stretched: site mirroring (2×) plus local protection per site. Usable = raw × (hosts − rebuild reserve) × (1 − operations reserve) × compression × dedupe ÷ policy. Global dedupe: ESA on VCF 9.1, 3–64 hosts, not stretched. Witness appliance (VCF 9.1): Medium 4 vCPU / 16 GB up to 500 VMs, Large 4 / 32.
  • Memory tiering (VCF 9): NVMe tier = DRAM × tier ratio (default 100%, up to 400%), capped by the drive and the 4 TB partition; two drives are mirrored (VCF 9.1). Tiering turns RAM overcommit off; keep active memory below half of DRAM.
  • Host types: cores per socket from current Intel Xeon (4th Gen to Xeon 6; 1, 2, 4 or 8 sockets) and AMD EPYC (9004/9005; 1 or 2 sockets); memory as balanced DDR5 configurations (8 or 12 channels per socket, 1 or 2 DIMMs per channel) up to vSphere's 24 TB per host; vSphere supports up to 960 logical CPUs per host.
  • Supervisor (vSphere Kubernetes Service): 3 control-plane VMs per Supervisor, Tiny 2 vCPU / 8 GB, Small 4 / 16, Medium 8 / 16, Large 16 / 32 (32 GB disk each). vSAN ESA ReadyNode guidance: at least 16 cores and 128 GB RAM per host, rising to 192 GB at 18 storage devices and 256 GB at 24. DR: "Protect to" adds the group's replica storage (and, with failover compute, its CPU and RAM) to the recovery cluster and counts its VMs as VMware Live Recovery protected VMs.
  • NSX Edge VMs: Small 2 vCPU / 4 GB, Medium 4 / 8, Large 8 / 32, XL 16 / 64 (200 GB each). Bare-metal edges are servers in the bill of materials, not ESX hosts, so they carry no VCF core licences.
  • Licensing: VCF per physical core, 16-core minimum per CPU, on every ESX host; 1 TiB of vSAN per VCF core, pooled; vSAN add-on TiB = raw capacity of all vSAN clusters (NVMe capacity drives) above that. vDefend Firewall / ATP per core of protected hosts (16-core minimum); Gateway Firewall per edge vCPU (bare metal: per core); Avi per Service Engine vCPU; VMware Live Recovery per protected VM. NSX, HCX, VCF Operations and VCF Automation are part of VCF. Data last verified 23 Nov 2026.
