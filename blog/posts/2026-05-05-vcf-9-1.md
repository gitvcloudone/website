Broadcom announced VCF 9.1, the first feature release since 9.0. It went GA later in May with an in-place upgrade from 9.0. The parts I'd look at first:

- Better memory tiering. You can turn it on without a reboot, and you can mirror the tier device in software.
- vSAN deduplication and compression on more cluster types.
- Live patching for ESX on TPM-enabled hosts, continuous compliance, and ransomware recovery on-prem.
- Bigger fleet and upgrade limits per VCF instance, faster VM and Kubernetes deployment, and S3 object storage as a tech preview.

9.1 is where memory tiering becomes practical for production. There's no reboot to enable it, and mirroring protects you if a tier drive fails without needing a RAID controller. It's also one of the two versions Amazon EVS added in July, so if you're on VCF at all, this is the release to plan around.
