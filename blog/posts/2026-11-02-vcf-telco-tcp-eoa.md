The standalone VMware Telco Cloud Platform (TCP) licensing SKUs reached end of availability on November 2, 2026. Telco customers are now covered under the VCF license and SKU, and the platform is called VMware Cloud Foundation Telco (VCF Telco).

TCP is no longer a separate product line. Both current telco features and upcoming ones are included in the VCF SKU. Since many operators already run VCF for IT, this puts virtual network functions (VNFs), cloud-native network functions (CNFs), IT workloads, and AI on one platform with one operating model instead of separate network and IT clouds.

VCF Telco includes:

- **Fleet management:** One management domain for IT and telco workloads from data center to edge, with zero-touch lifecycle management.
- **Kubernetes support:** Shared horizontal infrastructure for both VNFs and CNFs, with 2-year long-term support (LTS) on every minor Kubernetes release so operators can upgrade at their own pace.
- **Memory tiering and storage efficiency:** NVMe memory tiering that removes the hardware RAID dependency, plus compression with global deduplication to help lower server and storage costs.
- **Security and compliance:** Customer-controlled encryption keys, in-jurisdiction operations, and automated policy enforcement aligned to standards such as NIST and the UK Telecommunications Security Act. Zero-trust micro-segmentation is available as an option with VMware vDefend.

If you already run Telco Cloud Platform, you do not need to do anything right away. Current subscriptions and all of their components stay fully supported through the transition to VCF Telco, and at renewal your Broadcom representative will work with you to map your requirements to VCF. Existing VCF IT customers looking to add VCF Telco can work with their Broadcom account team to go over options. Before renewal comes up, ask your account team specifically how your current TCP entitlements map to the VCF SKU.
