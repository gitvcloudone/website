With SDDC 1.26v2, VMware Cloud on AWS supports a new host type, i7i.metal-24xl, for new SDDCs and existing ones.

**What the host is.** It runs on 5th Gen Intel Xeon (Emerald Rapids), with 48 physical cores and hyper-threading on by default, so vSphere sees 96 logical CPUs. It has 768 GiB of DDR5 memory, six 3.75 TB NVMe drives and 56.25 Gbps of networking. Broadcom estimates a three-host cluster gives about 13 TB usable on vSAN OSA and about 20 TB on vSAN ESA. The exact number depends on your storage policy and how well your data compresses.

**Where it fits.** Compared with i4i.metal, it's a smaller host with fewer cores on a newer, faster CPU. That suits workloads licensed per core, like SQL Server and Oracle. On secondary clusters you can also cap the cores used per host: 8, 16, 24, 30 or 36 on clusters of three or more hosts, and 16, 24, 30 or 36 on two-host clusters. Fewer licensed cores can save more than the host costs.

**Getting there.** Existing SDDCs must be on 1.26v2 first. A new SDDC defaults to vSAN ESA and can start at two hosts. Stretched clusters on i7i are opt-in and use vSAN OSA. To move from i3, i3en or i4i, add an i7i cluster and vMotion the VMs across, or ask for the cluster conversion service where it's eligible. Clusters already on VM hardware version 21 can't be converted from i4i to i7i.

If you're on i3 or i3en, this is the host to look at for your refresh. Check the Region list too: it's available in most major Regions, but not all of them.
