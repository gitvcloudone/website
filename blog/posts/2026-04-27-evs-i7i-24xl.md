Amazon EVS now supports i7i.metal-24xl, a smaller host than the i4i.metal it launched with, on a newer CPU.

How they compare:
- **i4i.metal:** 3rd Gen Intel Xeon (Ice Lake), 64 physical cores, 1,024 GiB of memory and eight 3.75 TB NVMe drives.
- **i7i.metal-24xl:** 5th Gen Xeon (Emerald Rapids), 48 physical cores, 768 GiB and six 3.75 TB drives.

AWS says i7i delivers up to 23% better compute performance and more than 10% better price performance than i4i.

**Why a smaller host helps.** VCF is subscribed per physical core, so on EVS your bill has two parts: the EC2 hosts and the VCF cores on them. A host with fewer but faster cores can do the same work while licensing fewer cores. That matters most for CPU-bound workloads, where the newer chip does more per core. Memory per core is the same on both, 16 GiB, so a memory-bound estate simply needs more i7i hosts than i4i hosts for the same total memory, and the licensing saving is smaller.

Run your own sizing both ways. For many estates, the i7i option comes out ahead on total cost once VCF cores are counted.
